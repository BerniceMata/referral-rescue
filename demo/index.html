Referral Rescue · a working prototype

Faxed referrals in. Scheduled patients out.

A primary care office faxes a referral to a specialist. If nobody reads it, the patient never books. This pipeline reads each fax, pulls out what scheduling needs, flags what's missing, drafts the follow-ups, and tracks every referral until the patient is seen.

All patients, providers and numbers below are synthetic. No real patient data is used anywhere.

Try it on six faxes

These are real outputs from the code, mistakes included. Three faxes use layouts the parser was tuned on (dev). Three use layouts written by someone who never saw the parser (held-out), which is the honest test of what happens when a new clinic's form shows up.

How accurate is it?

Field accuracy counts a field right only when it matches exactly after formatting is normalized (phone digits, ID characters). A "false fill" is the dangerous error: the system inventing a value for a field that was actually blank, which leads to a denied claim or a patient who is never reached. The rules version never did that on either set; when unsure, it leaves the field blank and flags it.

What the gap means. On familiar layouts the rules hit ; on layouts they had never seen they fell to , mostly on patient name, diagnosis and ICD-10 code, because a new form puts its labels in new places. That gap is why the next step is a vision model with structured output (written and wired into the same test, not yet run). It will be scored on the exact same held-out faxes, and the result will be published here whether it wins or loses.

How it works

Read the faxLocal text recognition on the CPU. Nothing leaves the machine, which matters for patient data.
ExtractPatient, date of birth, phone, insurance, member ID, specialty, diagnosis, ICD-10, urgency.
Flag what's missingBlank beats wrong. Missing fields get a drafted request back to the referring office.
Draft follow-upsA scheduling text to the patient and a missing-info fax. A person reviews and sends. Nothing auto-sends.
Track to "seen"An append-only log: received, needs info, contacted, scheduled, seen. Urgent referrals go overdue at 24 hours without contact.
